# Narration: AI for All · A Winter's Night in Limerick

Recorded in Boxi's own voice, cloned with ElevenLabs (eleven_v4; `tools/voice.py`). Files: `limerick/assets/voice/<id>.mp3`.
Window claims match `limerick/FACTS5.json`; the journey's electricity line rests on the CSO release in `limerick/FACTS2.json`.
No statistics. The narrator sets the scene and stops; the conversation on screen shows what happens. Keep the narrator's
English plain: Irish turns of phrase belong to the people in the bubbles, not to the narrator.
To re-record one clip, edit its text here and run `tools/voice.sh <id>`.
The `video_*` clips are only for the recorded film of the talk (`limerick.html?video`, see `VIDEO-LINES.md`): they fill
the stretches where Boxi would talk through each conversation in the room. The live talk never plays them.

## intro
A winter's night in Limerick, a few weeks before Christmas. Behind these windows, people are doing ordinary things: cooking, learning, remembering, keeping in touch. And quietly, AI is helping. Let's look inside.

## kitchen
Five o'clock, and dark already. Mary has lamb from the butcher, and the big old pot that was her mother's. Her mother never wrote the recipe down. So Mary takes a photo, and asks.

## nature
Seven o'clock. Something is singing out in the dark, under the light by the door. Tom opens the window, and holds up his phone.

## library
Half seven, at the library. Anne is learning something new. She asks it a question, then asks again, as many times as she needs. It never sighs. It sounds very sure of itself, and it isn't always right, so for the things that matter, she checks.

## travel
Eight o'clock. John and Kathleen are going to Italy in the spring, flying from Shannon. Tonight they're practising at home, with the guidebook and a phone.

## photos
Half eight, by the fire. Thousands of photos on Seán's phone, and his mother wants one: the dog, years ago, at the sea. He types two words.

## card
Nine o'clock. A card has come for Peggy from her granddaughter, and her eyes aren't what they were. She takes a photo of it with a free app.

## verse
Half nine. A Christmas card for her daughter in Australia has to go in the post this week, and the words won't come. Breda asks for a few lines, and then she asks again.

## weather
Last thing, the forecast. Behind it, Europe's weather scientists now run an AI forecast alongside their usual one, and the forecasters still decide what to tell us. Tomorrow, they say, looks bright.

## journey0
One last look. When Mary asked about her stew, where did her question go?

## journey1
It left her phone as a radio signal and reached a phone mast. From there it travelled as pulses of light, through fibre-optic cables under streets and fields.

## journey2
It arrived at a busy data centre, often far from where we live, its computers and cooling fans humming day and night.

## journey3
Inside are special chips. Each one does a huge number of small sums, all at once. That's how it works out an answer in a second or two.

## journey4
All of it runs on electricity, from wind farms and power stations, through the grid. Data centres use a large share of Ireland's electricity. That is a real cost, and it's worth knowing.

## journey5
And then the answer comes all the way back, to a kitchen in Limerick. A stew, one step at a time.

## finale
Eight windows, one winter's night. None of it is magic, and none of it replaces us. It helps us cook, learn, remember, and stay close. Let it help. But you decide.
